# Movement Feel v2 — Moveset Ladder, Game Feel, and Co-op Verbs

- **Prepared for:** THE BIG NAP (Godot 4.6 co-op 3D collectathon platformer, ages 5–9)
- **served_model:** claude-sonnet-5 (Sonnet 5), via Claude Agent SDK research subagent
- **Scope:** this file only — `docs/research/v2/movement_feel.md`. No other files were read for editing purposes; `core/movement/movement_tuning.gd`, `core/coop/carry_toss.gd`, and `GOAL.md` were read read-only for grounding, not modified.
- **Relationship to v1:** `docs/research/movement.md` already covers Godot `CharacterBody3D` fundamentals (coyote time, slopes, jump arc math, acceleration, ground-check nuance, 4.5/4.6 engine changes, starter parameter table). This file does not repeat that — it picks up one layer up: which *moves* belong on the ladder, which are kid-learnable vs parent-only, how AAA titles get feel and depth from small verb sets, how to make ground pound/glide *co-op* interactions, moving-platform velocity inheritance (the one Godot mechanic v1 didn't cover), and what an assist layer looks like when the base game already has no death.

`core/coop/carry_toss.gd`: the co-op verb already implemented is **asymmetric** — Otto (P2) carries Pip (P1); `p2_interact` picks up/tosses, `p1_interact` while carried always means "hop down gently"; `toss_up_velocity=7.5`, `toss_forward_velocity=3.0`, launch computed from Otto's facing. `GOAL.md` design floor (non-negotiable): "Input floor: single stick + jump + interact (extra verbs are ceiling, never floor; never invert controls)"; "no fail states, timers, death, or fall damage — gentle rescue instead"; "Progress lives in a persistent world that remembers across sessions — never skill-gated." This floor is the load-bearing constraint for every recommendation below: **any new verb must ride the existing stick/jump/interact chassis (tap vs. hold, context, or character-ownership — never a new button), and must never gate content behind having learned it.**

## 1. The moveset-ladder concept: Mario 64 vs. Odyssey vs. Astro Bot vs. the indies

**Mario 64** built depth through *jump chaining*: the triple jump requires three well-timed jumps in a row (run → jump → land → jump again → land → jump a third time for a high front flip), the long jump requires a run → crouch (Z) → jump within a tight window, and the dive can cancel any of the other jumps for horizontal distance. [Kevin's Super Mario 64 page](https://www.angelfire.com/games3/kevinsm64page/moves.html), [Triple Jump — Super Mario Wiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Triple_Jump), [Long Jump — Mario Wiki](https://mario.fandom.com/wiki/Long_Jump). This is deliberately **not** kid-floor-friendly: it's a timing-chain system, more verbs than THE BIG NAP's floor allows, and precisely the kind of multi-input sequencing that later sections show young kids struggle with.

**Super Mario Odyssey** solved the "too many verbs" problem differently: Cappy (throw/hold/hop off) is a *second, simpler input* that recontextualizes the *world* rather than adding permanent player verbs — capturing an enemy briefly grants that enemy's single ability (e.g. a Hammer Bro throwing hammers at cheese blocks), then it's gone. [What are devs saying about the design of Super Mario Odyssey?](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/what-are-devs-saying-about-the-design-of-i-super-mario-odyssey-i-) At GDC 2026, Odyssey's producer Kenta Motokura (reflecting on Odyssey while presenting Donkey Kong Bananza) framed this as "interactions primarily involving approaching and playing with objects in the world" rather than expanding a fixed player moveset. [Shacknews GDC 2026 recap](https://www.shacknews.com/article/148276/donkey-kong-bananza-gdc-2026-panel-recap). This is the *contextual gadget* pattern — verbs live in the world, not in a permanent skill tree.

**Astro Bot** is the sharpest precedent for THE BIG NAP: Astro's entire base kit is **jump, hover, punch, spin-attack** — four verbs, deliberately minimal — and the game became the highest-rated title of 2024 and swept Game of the Year. [Astro Bot — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astro_Bot). Reviewers were explicit that depth came from *polish and level design*, not verb count: "Jumping, hovering, punching, and spinning all feels spot on thanks to tight, responsive controls... the true test of any 3D platformer is whether it feels fun to simply move around, and Astro passes handily." [Push Square review](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/astro-bot). Crucially, Astro Bot does **not** permanently expand this base kit — stage-specific *gadgets* (a jump-boosting dog backpack, boxing gloves, etc.) grant a temporary extra verb for that level only, and level design is built around the gadget. [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/astro-bot). Team Asobi's own framing: they wanted "a game that feels like a toy," where every action gets an immediate reaction, and internal pacing during a level explicitly cycles a rhythm like "punch, punch, jump, punch, punch, jump" tuned around the music. [Astro Bot review roundup](https://seasonedgaming.com/2024/09/11/review-astro-bot-goat-tier-robot-adventures/); [CEDEC 2025 / NeoGAF summary of Hironori Yatoku's level-design talk](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/4gamer-cedec-2025-the-secret-to-the-fast-paced-3d-level-design-of-astro-bot-how-team-asobi-designed-the-user-experience-by-hironori-yatoku.1686691/).

**A Hat in Time / Penny's Big Breakaway** — the indie 3D-platformer wave — used a **"verb sketch" design process**. Penny's Big Breakaway designer Esteban Fajardo literally drew sketches of candidate verbs early; "throw," "sleep," "ride," and "swing" (yo-yo actions) survived from first sketch to shipped game. [Inside the Dazzling Design of Penny's Big Breakaway — Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/02/16/inside-the-dazzling-design-of-3d-platformer-pennys-big-breakaway/). The stated design pillar was **"expression"**: maximize what a player can *do* with a small, mastered core moveset (all yo-yo verbs live on one analog stick, no thumb-lifting required) rather than adding buttons. Same Xbox Wire source.

**Takeaway for THE BIG NAP:** the Astro Bot model (small fixed base kit + polish + *contextual, non-permanent* gadget verbs) is the correct fit for the GOAL.md floor ("extra verbs are ceiling, never floor... never skill-gated"). Mario 64's jump-chain model and Penny's stick-based yo-yo expression are both too input-dense for a 5-year-old's floor, but their *design process* (verb sketching before implementation) is worth adopting as a discipline.

## 2. What's kid-learnable at 5–9 vs. parent-only

Hard developmental data specific to platformer inputs is thin, but the adjacent research is informative and should be read cautiously (it's about generalized motor-cognitive tasks, not platformer button-mapping specifically):

- Basic jumping (off structures, from standing) is a **2–3 year old** milestone; sprint and countermovement-jump *physical* performance develops steadily from age 5 through 14, plateauing roughly 9–12. [Kinematic/Kinetic Development of Sprinting and Jump Performance in Boys](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7676893/) — this is about the body, not the controller, but it's the floor under everything else.
- Motor-cognitive coupling — integrating predictive models, sensory feedback, and top-down inhibitory control for *online correction* of a movement already in flight (exactly what a wall-jump chain or a frame-perfect double-jump demands) does not approach adult-like performance until roughly **11–12 years old**. [Persistence of motor-cognitive inhibition deficits in children with DCD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12756298/); [Motor-cognitive coupling in children with DCD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10628065/). **Read with caution**: this is clinical/developmental-coordination-disorder literature on generalized anti-jump/inhibition tasks, not platformer research — treat it as directional evidence, not a hard cutoff, but it does support the general design instinct that *timing-critical, multi-input chains* (frame-perfect double jumps, wall-jump ladders) are the wrong floor for a 5–9 audience and belong, if at all, on the ceiling.
- Coordination-demanding play is *positively linked* to conceptual/cognitive development in preschoolers when the activity also demands cognitive engagement — i.e., movement games aren't just physically fine for young kids, they may be developmentally productive, provided the cognitive load is age-appropriate. [Coordinative/dynamic strength and conceptual thinking in preschoolers](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10973122/).
- Shipped-game evidence for what a AAA studio considers kid-floor: **Kirby and the Forgotten Land** keeps controls deliberately flat — one dedicated button each for attack/absorb, jump, ability-release, and item-use, with both L and R doing the same thing (Crouch Guard) rather than assigning different functions per shoulder button — explicitly cited as approachable for beginner and kid players. [CGMagazine: Best Switch Game for Beginner Kids](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/parental-no-scope/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-best-switch-kids/); [Kirby and the Forgotten Land controls guide — Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/129470/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-controls-guide). Its co-op partner, Bandana Waddle Dee (P2), has a moveset whose *strength scales with Kirby's own progress* rather than being separately leveled, and P2 never forces a restart on P1's death. [Common Sense Media review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land).

**Design rule extracted:** kid-floor verbs are single-button, hold-not-mash, non-timing-critical, and forgiving of "too early / too late" input (this is exactly what coyote time and jump buffering already do for THE BIG NAP's single jump). Anything that requires *chaining* two inputs within a tight window, or correcting a trajectory mid-air based on fast feedback, is parent-ceiling material, not child-floor material.

## 3. Game feel: Swink, juice, and AAA feel-engineering

Steve Swink's *Game Feel* (2009) frames "feel" as the intersection of **real-time response, simulated space, and polish** — and devotes case-study attention to graphing Mario 64's jump physics specifically as the canonical example of tunable jump feel. [Game Feel — Taylor & Francis](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.1201/9781482267334/game-feel-steve-swink); [Liz England's review of Game Feel](https://lizengland.com/blog/review-game-feel-by-steve-swink/); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/3385050-game-feel). The three-part frame maps directly onto THE BIG NAP's existing systems: *real-time response* = the buffer/coyote windows already in `movement_tuning.gd`; *simulated space* = the derived-gravity apex-hang curve (`apex_hang_threshold`, `apex_gravity_mult`); *polish* = the squash/stretch juice layer already implemented.

The canonical "juice" GDC talk — Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho's **"Juice it or Lose it"** — takes a bare block-breaker and iteratively layers particles, screenshake, sound, and animation, demonstrating each layer's individual contribution to feel. [Fave Game Talks (Juice/Feel) playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL2gEO25pE6dqsPxgajrZSuqutgzZSjnk5); [Squeezing more juice out of your game design — Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/squeezing-more-juice-out-of-your-game-design-). The applicable pattern for camera work: **a decaying shake offset where anything can request a shake and the strongest request wins** — a normal hop asks for almost nothing, a hard landing asks for a lot. [Coding Quests: Game Juice in Godot 4](https://codingquests.io/blog/godot-4-game-juice-platformer). This is a one-function addition (`camera.request_shake(amplitude)`, decaying each frame, max() against current) that composes cleanly with the existing squash/stretch spring in `movement_tuning.gd`.

Astro Bot's own feel-engineering philosophy, per Team Asobi: "a game that feels like a toy" — every action gets an immediate, legible reaction; physics-based interactions determine whether a jump feels "crisp" or "mushy," and whether an object has real weight or is just scenery. [NookGaming review](https://www.nookgaming.com/astro-bot-review-celebrating-30-years-of-play/); GDC Vault also hosts an earlier, directly relevant talk on the original PSVR Astro Bot's engineering: [Taming Technologies Behind 'ASTRO BOT Rescue Mission' — GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026072/Taming-Technologies-Behind-ASTRO-BOT) (vault access, not fully fetchable, but confirmed to exist and be on-topic).

## 4. Ground pound as co-op interaction; asymmetric verbs (It Takes Two, Kirby, DKC)

**It Takes Two** is the reference case for asymmetric co-op *forcing* cooperation without a fail-state stick: each of the two playable characters (Cody/May) gets structurally different abilities per chapter, "keeps co-op from becoming 'two people doing the same thing,' and prevents one player from carrying the entire experience for long stretches." [Medium: It Takes Two — how a game truly demonstrates cooperation](https://medium.com/@longvuahs/it-takes-two-how-a-game-truly-demonstrates-cooperation-of-2-players-592ebf1b3caa). There is no single-player mode by design — both players must synchronize on timing-based switches and role-specific actions. Same source. THE BIG NAP already has the seed of this in `carry_toss.gd` (Otto carries, Pip is carried) — it is currently a *pickup/toss* relationship, not yet a *verb* asymmetry.

**Ground pound as a bounce mechanic** has direct precedent: in the Galaxy games, Orange Springboards marked with the Ground Pound icon fling the player into the air when pounded — a *pound-to-launch* interaction. [Ground Pound — Super Mario Wiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Ground_Pound). Search evidence on co-op partner abilities in the DKC/Luigi's Mansion lineage reinforces the "each character owns a different half of the interaction" pattern: Diddy's hover, Dixie's lift, Cranky's cane bounce, or (Luigi's Mansion 3) one character opens a path while the other exploits it. General synthesis, no single primary source, cross-referenced against the Mario Wiki ground pound entry above.

**Academic grounding for family-specific asymmetric design**: a paper titled *"Promoting Family Play through Asymmetric Game Design"* argues that asymmetric and asynchronous mechanics can reconcile a child's and an adult's differing capabilities within the same co-op session, using **interdependent gameplay mechanics** (each player's actions are necessary but not sufficient — "your victory condition only completes when theirs does too") specifically to foster real cross-generational interaction rather than one player carrying the other. [Promoting Family Play through Asymmetric Game Design (PDF)](https://techandpeople.github.io/downloads/Promoting_Family_Play_through_Asymmetric_Game_Design.pdf) — note: this PDF could not be parsed to text by the available fetch tooling in this session; the claim above is drawn from the search-engine abstract/snippet, not a full read, and should be treated as a secondary-source summary pending a direct read.

**Kirby Forgotten Land's Bandana Waddle Dee** (P2) is the clean existing shipped precedent closest to Otto/Pip: a spear-wielding partner whose "strength scales with Kirby's" own progression (no separate leveling UI, no way to fall behind) and whose presence removes death-restart pressure from the game entirely. [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land). Since THE BIG NAP's floor already has no death, the analogous risk to guard against is *ability* imbalance rather than survival imbalance — i.e., make sure Otto and Pip's asymmetric verbs are equally *fun to be*, not equally *powerful*.

## 5. Godot: moving-platform velocity inheritance (the gap v1 didn't cover)

v1's `movement.md` covers coyote time, slopes, jump arc, acceleration, ground-check, and 4.5/4.6 engine deltas, but not moving platforms — relevant for Phase 5 world-building. Current best-practice consensus for Godot 4 `CharacterBody3D`:

- Use **`AnimatableBody3D`** for the platform (not `StaticBody3D`, not `RigidBody3D`), with **`sync_to_physics = true`** so its velocity is correctly reported to bodies standing on it. [Fix: Godot CharacterBody3D Moving Platform Not Following — Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-godot-character-body-3d-moving-platform-not-following); [Godot Forum: Inheriting platform velocity on rigidbody character controller](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/inheriting-platform-velocity-on-rigidbody-character-controller/87953).
- The character must set `platform_floor_layers` to match the platform's collision layer (commonly `0xFFFFFFFF` for "all layers" during greybox, narrowed later), and should set **`platform_on_leave` = `PLATFORM_ON_LEAVE_ADD_VELOCITY`** so a jump taken while riding a moving platform preserves the platform's momentum at launch — the other enum values intentionally discard it. Same Bugnet source; [Godot CharacterBody2D/3D Complete Guide](https://godot-mcp.abyo.net/guides/godot4-characterbody).
- **Critical gotcha**: velocity inheritance is computed *inside* `move_and_slide()` itself — if a script ever moves the character manually (bypassing `move_and_slide()` for a frame, e.g. during the carry/toss reparenting in `carry_toss.gd`) platform-riding will silently break for that frame. Worth a defensive comment/test given THE BIG NAP already reparents Pip onto Otto's `CarrySocket` and back.

## 6. Assist layer when the base game already has no death

Since THE BIG NAP's floor already removes death/fail-states via bubble rescue, the two shipped assist-mode precedents both suggest the *remaining* assist surface is about **independence and readability**, not survival:

- **Super Mario Odyssey's Assist Mode**: beyond the bubble-rescue-on-hazard mechanic THE BIG NAP already has as its *base* behavior, Odyssey Assist Mode's other levers are (a) never drowning, (b) idle regen, and — most transferable here — **visual guide arrows** that appear in the world pointing toward the next objective, escalating to an arrow above Mario's own head if the player strays far from the path. [Assist Mode — Super Mario Odyssey Wiki](https://smo.wiki/Assist_Mode); [Dualshockers: Odyssey accessibility options](https://www.dualshockers.com/super-mario-odyssey-accessibility-options/). Framed explicitly as adding options with **"no penalty to using it"** — it doesn't reduce what a skilled player can do, it just removes friction for one who needs it. Same sources.
- **Celeste's Assist Mode** is the industry's most-cited "accessibility without removing the game" case study: sliders for game speed, invincibility, infinite stamina, and chapter-skip, framed by its designers with the analogy "a wheelchair ramp doesn't ruin stairs... assist mode doesn't ruin a game." [Game Accessibility Guidelines: Celeste assist mode](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/celeste-assist-mode/); [Vice: The Small But Important Change Celeste Made to Assist Mode](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-assist-mode-change-and-accessibility/).

**Applied to THE BIG NAP**, where death/fail is already off the table, the assist layer should target: (1) an Odyssey-style **guide arrow** for a 4–5-year-old who gets lost in 3D space (a known camera-readability risk per this project's own `camera_readability.md`), (2) a **toss-arc preview line** for Pip's launch trajectory the parent can toggle on for a child still building spatial prediction, and (3) an optional **auto-recenter-camera** assist (distinct from the mandatory auto-camera floor) that more aggressively snaps behind the child's character during solo/buddy-AI play. None of these touch the input floor or add a fail-state — they're pure legibility scaffolding, matching Odyssey and Celeste's "no penalty, no removed challenge" framing.

# TOP 12 ACTIONABLE

A concrete moveset ladder for THE BIG NAP, ordered from "should exist now" to "world-contextual ceiling," with feel specs. Every item respects the GOAL.md floor: no new permanent buttons, nothing skill-gated, tap/hold/context only.

1. **Lock the base kit at 4 verbs, Astro Bot-style.** Move (camera-relative stick), Jump (tap), Interact/Carry-Toss (context), and a to-be-designed 4th "toy" verb — no more. Astro Bot shipped GOTY on jump/hover/punch/spin; resist adding a 5th permanent verb even under design pressure. [Astro Bot — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astro_Bot).

2. **Give Jump a hold-to-extend "float" behavior riding the existing apex-hang system, not a new button.** `movement_tuning.gd` already has `apex_hang_threshold=0.5` and `apex_gravity_mult=0.4`. Spec: while jump is *held* (not tapped) and vertical speed is within the apex-hang band, extend `apex_gravity_mult` application for up to **+0.35s** before falling back to `fall_gravity_mult=1.55`. This is a Astro-Bot-hover-style "temporarily float through the air" verb (["laser thrusters cut out after a couple seconds" — Game8](https://game8.co/games/Astro-Bot/archives/471580)) built entirely from tap-vs-hold on the one button that already exists — zero new input floor.

3. **Make ground pound Otto's (the carrier's) verb, triggered by hold-jump-then-press-interact while airborne** — one new *context*, zero new buttons. This gives Otto a "heavy" identity (break/bounce interactions) distinct from Pip's float, riding the same GOAL.md-compliant tap/hold/context rule as item 2.

4. **Ground-pound-to-bounce, tuned off the existing toss numbers.** `carry_toss.gd` already establishes `toss_up_velocity=7.5` as the game's "gentle launch" reference. Spec a pound-bounce (for hitting a spring pad, or bouncing Pip if Pip is standing nearby) at **9.0–10.0 m/s** vertical — noticeably punchier than a toss, still well under `terminal_velocity=20.0`. Precedent: Galaxy's Ground-Pound Orange Springboards. [Mario Wiki: Ground Pound](https://www.mariowiki.com/Ground_Pound).

5. **Deepen Otto/Pip into a true asymmetric pair, not just carry-toss.** Otto = grounded/heavy (ground pound, push heavy props). Pip = airborne/light (the float from item 2, can be tossed *through* gaps Otto can't reach). This is the It Takes Two pattern — "keeps co-op from becoming two people doing the same thing" — applied without adding inputs, since both new verbs (float, pound) are hold/context variants of the single jump/interact pair each character already owns. [Medium: It Takes Two cooperation analysis](https://medium.com/@longvuahs/it-takes-two-how-a-game-truly-demonstrates-cooperation-of-2-players-592ebf1b3caa).

6. **New verbs are world gadgets, never permanent unlocks.** Follow Astro Bot's dog-backpack/boxing-glove pattern: a "4th verb" (glide flower, bounce shroom, whatever the world fiction is) should be picked up/left in a specific world area, not banked to a save-file skill tree. This is the only way to add ceiling content while satisfying GOAL.md's "never skill-gated" floor. [Push Square: Astro Bot gadget design](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/astro-bot).

7. **Keep every new verb single-input, hold-not-mash, non-timing-critical.** No wall-jump chains, no frame-perfect double-jumps, no input sequences. This is the direct design consequence of the motor-cognitive-maturity research (inhibitory control / online trajectory correction not adult-like until ~11–12) — treat as directional caution, not a hard citation-backed cutoff, but it agrees with Kirby Forgotten Land's shipped one-button-per-function floor. [Kirby Forgotten Land controls — Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/129470/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-controls-guide); [DCD motor-cognitive coupling](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10628065/).

8. **Add camera-shake-on-impact using the "strongest request wins" pattern.** One function: `request_shake(amplitude: float)`, decaying each frame, new requests only override if larger. Wire it to landing velocity (already computed for squash) and to the pound-bounce in item 4. Cheap, and directly cited as core to platformer "juice." [Coding Quests: Game Juice in Godot 4](https://codingquests.io/blog/godot-4-game-juice-platformer); [Juice it or Lose it talk playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL2gEO25pE6dqsPxgajrZSuqutgzZSjnk5).

9. **Give ground pound its own particle/dust signature distinct from a normal landing's squash-poof**, so a 5-year-old can *read* which verb just happened from the screen alone (Swink's "simulated space" legibility). Reuses the existing squash/stretch spring infrastructure in `movement_tuning.gd`; just needs a second, larger particle burst gated on pound vs. regular landing.

10. **Coyote/buffer numbers already validated — no change needed.** Current `coyote_time=0.20s` / `jump_buffer=0.22s` sit at or above the most forgiving published tier for casual/family platformers (coyote 110–170ms, buffer 120–180ms per synthesized industry ranges vs. tight-precision platformers' 70–100/70–110ms). [Input Buffering and Coyote Time primer](https://gamineai.com/blog/input-buffering-and-coyote-time-in-2d-a-godot-4-and-unity-friendly-timing-primer). This confirms the existing tuning is correctly generous for the 5–9 audience — flag as validated, not a to-do.

11. **Implement moving platforms now, correctly, before Phase 5 worlds need them.** `AnimatableBody3D` + `sync_to_physics=true` on the platform; character sets `platform_floor_layers` to match and `platform_on_leave = PLATFORM_ON_LEAVE_ADD_VELOCITY`. Add a regression test/comment guarding against `carry_toss.gd`'s socket-reparenting ever skipping a `move_and_slide()` frame, since that's the one place in the current codebase that already manually repositions a body. [Bugnet: Fix Godot CharacterBody3D Moving Platform Not Following](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-godot-character-body-3d-moving-platform-not-following).

12. **Build the assist layer as legibility scaffolding, not a difficulty slider — because there's no difficulty to lower.** Three concrete, floor-respecting, parent-toggleable additions: (a) an Odyssey-style guide arrow toward the current objective, (b) a toss-arc preview line while Otto is aiming a toss, (c) a more aggressive auto-recenter camera mode for solo/buddy-AI play. Framed the way both Odyssey and Celeste frame their assist layers: **"no penalty for using it, doesn't remove what a confident player can do."** [Assist Mode — Super Mario Odyssey Wiki](https://smo.wiki/Assist_Mode); [Game Accessibility Guidelines: Celeste](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/celeste-assist-mode/).
